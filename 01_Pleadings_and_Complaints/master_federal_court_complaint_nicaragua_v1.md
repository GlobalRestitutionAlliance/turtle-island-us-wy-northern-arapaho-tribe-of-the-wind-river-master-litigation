## COMPLAINT FOR POST-JUDGMENT ENFORCEMENT, RESTITUTION, AND CONSTRUCTIVE TRUST

Plaintiff, the Republic of Nicaragua, by and through designated legal counsel, brings this civil action against the United States of America, the Department of Defense, the Central Intelligence Agency, the Department of State, and the Department of the Treasury (collectively, "Defendants"), and alleges upon personal knowledge, official state archives, declassified records, and final judicial records as follows:

---

### I. NATURE OF THE ACTION

1. This is a civil action seeking:
   a. Domestication, confirmation, and post-judgment enforcement of the final, non-appealable Judgment on the Merits rendered on 27 June 1986 by the **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** in *Military and Paramilitary Activities in and against Nicaragua (Nicaragua v. United States of America)*, I.C.J. Reports 1986, p. 14;
   b. Commercial asset garnishment and execution pursuant to the Foreign Sovereign Immunities Act, 28 U.S.C. §§ 1605(a)(3) and 1610(a)(6), arising from Defendants' bad-faith sovereign default under Article 94(2) of the Charter of the United Nations;
   c. Liquidated and compensatory damages for unadjudicated historical maritime spoliations, unlawful military occupations, and customs house receiverships spanning 172 continuous years (1854–2026); and
   d. The immediate establishment of a court-supervised **Qualified Settlement Fund (QSF) Trust** pursuant to 26 U.S.C. § 468B to receive, preserve, and distribute restitution proceeds for the direct benefit of the injured Nicaraguan citizenry and national infrastructure.

2. Total certified damages across the 25 actionable instruments set forth in this action equal **$250,000,000,000.00 USD ($250.00 Billion)**.

### II. JURISDICTION AND VENUE

3. Subject-matter jurisdiction is conferred upon this Court pursuant to:
   a. **28 U.S.C. § 1331** (Federal Question Jurisdiction), as this action arises under treaties of the United States, including the Charter of the United Nations (59 Stat. 1031, T.S. No. 993) and customary international law as incorporated into federal common law;
   b. **28 U.S.C. § 1350** (Alien Tort Statute), as this action seeks tort damages for violations of the law of nations and treaties of the United States;

   d. **28 U.S.C. § 2201–2202** (Declaratory Judgment Act), authorizing this Court to declare the enforceability of binding international adjudications.

4. Venue is proper in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia pursuant to **28 U.S.C. § 1391(e)(1)**, as Defendants maintain their principal official headquarters within this judicial district, and a substantial part of the operational orders, funding authorizations, and diplomatic directives giving rise to Plaintiff's claims occurred in this District.

### IV. FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

#### A. The 1986 International Court of Justice Adjudication
8. On 9 April 1984, Plaintiff instituted proceedings against Defendant United States before the International Court of Justice concerning military and paramilitary activities in and against Nicaragua.
9. Defendant United States participated fully in the jurisdictional phase of the proceedings, arguing that the Court lacked jurisdiction. On 26 November 1984, the ICJ rejected Defendant's preliminary objections by an overwhelming majority (15 to 1), finding conclusively that it possessed jurisdiction to adjudicate the dispute under Article 36 of the ICJ Statute.
10. Defendant subsequently withdrew from further participation in bad faith, but under Article 53 of the ICJ Statute, such withdrawal did not impair the validity of the proceedings or the finality of the Court's judgment.
11. On 27 June 1986, the ICJ rendered its final Judgment on the Merits, holding:
    a. Defendant unlawfully laid mines in Nicaraguan internal and territorial waters during 1984, injuring commercial vessels and blockading lawful sea commerce;
    b. Defendant unlawfully attacked Nicaraguan ports, oil installations, and civilian infrastructure (Corinto, Puerto Sandino, Potosí);
    c. Defendant unlawfully trained, armed, equipped, and financed paramilitary forces (*Contras*) to overthrow the lawful government of Nicaragua; and
    d. Defendant breached its obligations under customary international law not to use force, not to intervene in the internal affairs of another sovereign State, and not to violate sovereign territorial integrity.
12. In Operative Paragraph 292(13), the ICJ ruled conclusively that Defendant **is under an obligation to make reparation to the Republic of Nicaragua for all injury caused to Nicaragua by the breaches of obligations under customary international law**.

#### B. The 172-Year Historical Continuum (1854–2026)
13. The 1986 Judgment represents the culmination of 172 continuous years of documented, unprovoked armed aggression and economic subjugation by Defendant against Plaintiff:
    a. **1854 Greytown Destruction:** The unprovoked bombardment and burning of the civilian trading port of San Juan del Norte by the *USS Cyane*;
    b. **1855–1857 Walker Filibuster Usurpation:** Defendant's privateers seized the Nicaraguan presidency and re-instituted slavery;
    c. **1909 Knox Note Overthrow:** Direct diplomatic coercion and naval landings compelling the overthrow of President José Santos Zelaya;
    d. **1912–1933 Marine Military Occupations:** Direct military rule and confiscation of national customs revenues on behalf of Wall Street banking syndicates;
    e. **1914 Bryan-Chamorro Treaty Usurpation:** Extorted perpetual transit monopoly, declared null and void *ab initio* by the Central American Court of Justice in 1916 and 1917.
